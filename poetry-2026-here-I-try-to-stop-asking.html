Here I try to stop asking

Poetry · Spring 2026
    Tell me what I’m missing, and why

    a bed of clover still makes me gentle and soothed.

    If there are duck bones at the bottom of the pond,

    or a buck with an arrow in its neck, yellow-bellied toad,

    a hen carcass left by a fox in the run,

    a beehive knocked down,

    a bear cub, did I know the weeping willow or not—

    it was humid or cold

    and there was tea in the sun it tasted like dust

    black raspberries and basil a salamander once

    I walked through the blade sharp sprinkler spray

    didn’t I—

    I go barefoot down and back on sour hulls

    crushed among the thistle I pulled up but with gloves—

    pulling it along spiny fuschia flowers and a mat of white taproots

    tangled browned and clinging to the shy side of the hill

    my toes reddened are grabbing clay just like that

    under the lilac tree breathing also 

    just like that do you see

    the firepit ash

    the willow
