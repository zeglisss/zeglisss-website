To grow

Poetry · 2026

Rhubarb ripens under overturned terracotta and lemon balm spreads along the reach of the bed. Clouds drop heavy onto the treetops and drip hill to hill. We call it fog. We walk barefoot on cut grass and headless dandelions. We amputate trees when they break on their own in the storm. You get what you get is a powerful phrase. Also, it is what it is. Which is why tree petals ride the breeze and forget being leaves. Sometimes dreams are wishes and sometimes they are prophecies, so I always assumed my first instincts were dangerous. I like the taste of acetone and the smell of filing my nails too fast. I say the important stuff piece by piece but can’t promise it will be in order. For example, warm summer night air crammed with cotton-balls. By coincidence, heat lightning.

Back when my hands were bigger than my feet I stood in closets and chewed handfuls of ice. I could do a back bend. I asked and answered until everything was hot to the touch. I tasted red I choked it down it came back up. I got what I got. Walk on my knees. It is what it is. My path is unfamiliar so my footprints last like damage. They needed to call it screaming bloody murder so I learned that violence is a thing you do to yourself. I regret saying sorry so often like how a well-received couch gets old fast. Nobody stayed in the room with me so I turned red and stayed that way. You can only swallow empty several hundred times in a row.

I thought all of me would grow at one pace together but my tongue got strong first and my feet got wide last. I became that type of woman that keeps track. It is what it is. I cross my fingers the wrong way and clap with my palms turned out. I got what I got. Lately in my sleep I make it far into the forest. I make it to running water. I dance between the webbed roots like a staircase kicking forward and back like how you flung your foot when your knee felt funny.
