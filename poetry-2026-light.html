Light

Poetry · Spring 2026
    It hits the side of the gate and the gate turns yellow. 
    The yellow hits the gold of my eye and I know 
    then, we've reached four-thirty. I am a sun
    dial, you are the sun. 
    
    Four-thirty means it has been a week. 
    The week makes a month, the month knits
    into a year which sings a different story altogether. 
    
    At the end of the scarf, there I am 
    knee-deep on carpet on concrete 
    and knocking on the baseboards. 
    Baseboards made of plastic. 
    Yarn made of wool pulled from my throat, 
    wet and red-clotted, the longest it will get. 
    Being stretched, it pops out 
    of the joint, back in again, firmly pressed. 
    
    It collides pink, now, cloud flesh. 
    Just longer than a week, the month swells 
    to fit. There, at the end of the tangle, wandering
    finds me on a Tuesday night. Soap and cologne 
    say presence, but the handle won't turn. 
    
    I keep looking, hinges, door viewer, grey, 
    waiting on something that left last night, 
    sifting through hallways and being 
    not alone, but solely within this wedge of space. 
    
    It is light, and it knows itself. Time only tells time. 
    Reflection asks for patience, everything 
    will turn yellow. I wish I could jump into the sky 
    and fall. Most light fades at night, pinpricks stay, 
    bleeding out and down to both of us, 
    under one blanket where your hand grips mine,
    which is no longer a hand, but a thing you held 
    onetime.
